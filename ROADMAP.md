## Findings so far (from `python src/analysis.py`)
- 1,121 Leverkusen build-ups from their own third: **55% reached the final third**, 14% ended in a shot, only **3.4% were lost in their own third**.
- They exit evenly across all five channels (18–23% each), so there's no single side to "show" them to. Xhaka (107), Wirtz (94) and Grimaldo (76) receive most exits.
- **Press height matters a lot:** correlation of −0.74 between how high opponents pressed and how often Leverkusen got up the pitch. Bayern twice held them to 25–30%.
- **Scorelines hide how well some presses worked:** see the case study below. Frankfurt lost 5-1 but held Leverkusen to 0.99 open-play xG, below their usual 1.51.
- **Most press-resistant:** Xhaka (7% loss rate under pressure), Stanišić, Palacios, Tah, Tapsoba. **Most likely to lose it:** Boniface, Frimpong, Tella, Hofmann (16–23%).
- Goal kick 360 data covers only 55 kicks, so treat Figure 4 as a starting point, not a finding.

## Phase 4 findings: xT and turnovers
- **xT model** (16x12 grid, fitted on all 34 matches): having the ball in the central zone right in front of goal is worth 0.41; in Leverkusen's own half, every zone is under 0.02. Limitation: fitted on 34 matches involving one team, so it partly reflects Leverkusen's own style. A full-league fit would be cleaner.
- **Most dangerous exit: the left half-space** (38 xT per 1,000 build-ups, n=161), then the centre (33). **Least dangerous: both wings** (23–24), although the left wing is their most-used exit (183).
- **Carries out of the third** produce more threat (32) than short passes (26), and 78% of them reach the final third. Exits through Tapsoba lead to the most xT of any player (41 per 1,000, n=43), and exits through Stanišić reach the final third most often (84%).
- **Turnovers:** 144 open-play losses in their own half after build-ups; 28 (19%) led to an opponent shot, totalling 3.3 xG over the season. **The left wing is where they lose it most (41)**, against 25 on the right.
- **Who loses it:** Wirtz 18, Hrádecký 14 (long or forced passes from the goalkeeper), Grimaldo 14, Xhaka 14, Tapsoba 13. Note that Xhaka's count reflects how much of the ball he has, not carelessness (see Figure 3).

### What this suggests for the recommendation (check it on video in Phase 3)
1. **Block the inside channels:** block passes into the left half-space and centre, where their exits are worth most.
2. **Show them to their left touchline:** wide exits carry the least threat, and the left wing is where they give the ball away most.
3. **Stop the centre-backs carrying:** step a forward onto Tapsoba (and Stanišić when he plays) as they carry out, rather than sitting off them.
4. **Caveat:** Grimaldo is a big attacking threat once he's past the first line, so "showing them left" only works if the press arrives as he receives.

## Phase 4 findings: Bayern case study (Figures 8–10)
Labels are from the opponent's side: Bayern (H) 2-2 = Bayern at home, drew 2-2; Bayern (A) 0-3 = Bayern away, lost 3-0.

**Correction to the starting question.** We assumed Frankfurt and Bochum were "carved open". In open play, only Bochum were.

| | Bayern (H) 2-2 | Bayern (A) 0-3 | Frankfurt (H) 1-5 | Bochum (A) 0-4 | Other 30 (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leverkusen build-ups reaching final third | 30% | 26% | 51% | 52% | 56% |
| Their high pressures that won the ball within 5s | 20% | 15% | 20% | 21% | 14% |
| ...and kept it (started their own possession within 5s) | 12.5% | 13.5% | 15.7% | **6.5%** | 5.3% |
| Their xG from possessions won in Leverkusen's half | 1.32 | 0.50 | 1.05 | **0.17** | 0.31 |
| Leverkusen open-play xG | 1.37 | 0.54 | 0.99 | **1.83** | 1.51 |
| Their pressures in Leverkusen's defensive third | 40 | 52 | 51 | **77** | 35 |
| Leverkusen exits played long | 24% | 15% | **32%** | 21% | 15% |

1. **All four pressed well enough to win the ball.** Each won it back from 15–21% of their high pressures, above the usual 14%.
2. **Bayern and Frankfurt kept it; Bochum gave it straight back.** Bayern and Frankfurt turned most of those regains into their own possession (12.5–15.7% of pressures). Bochum won the ball just as often (21%), but kept it only 6.5% of the time, about a normal opponent's rate. Leverkusen won it straight back and played through them: 1.83 open-play xG, and only 0.17 xG for Bochum from regains.
3. **Bayern stopped the build-up at source.** Leverkusen reached the final third in only 26–30% of build-ups (season median 56%). At home (2-2), Bayern also created 1.32 xG from regains.
4. **Frankfurt's press worked, but Leverkusen went over it.** Leverkusen played long out of their third more than in any other match (32%), so they still got up the pitch, but created only 0.99 open-play xG (usual: 1.51). Frankfurt lost 5-1 through 2 penalties and Leverkusen scoring 3 open-play goals from under 1 xG: finishing, not the build-up.
5. **Penalties mattered a lot.** Of Leverkusen's 14 goals in the four matches: 7 from open play or counters, 4 penalties, and 1 each from a throw-in, corner and free kick (Figure 10). Restarts only count if the shot came within 10 seconds: StatsBomb tags a whole possession by how it started, so a goal 20 passes after a throw-in is really open play.
6. **360 data didn't separate them.** Opponents committed into Leverkusen's half (4.7–5.7 players) and distance to the nearest opponent were similar across all four.

**What this means for the recommendation:** winning the ball is not enough; the press needs a plan for the first pass after the regain. Bochum's problem was not their press but what happened after it. Check on video (Phase 3): were Bochum's regains loose touches with no support close by?

**Limitations:** 4 matches is a small sample. "Won the ball" = a ball recovery, won interception or won tackle by the pressing team within 5 seconds. "Kept it" = StatsBomb opened a possession for them within 5 seconds, which requires control of the ball. I first used only the second measure and wrongly concluded Bochum's press didn't win the ball. Open-play xG excludes penalties and shots within 10 seconds of a restart.

## Phase 4 findings: pass network and Xhaka (Figures 11–12)
**Normal build-up (32 matches):** Xhaka is the hub (he makes or receives 27% of all completed own-half passes), with Tah (25%) and Tapsoba (20%) either side. The strongest links are Xhaka–Tah (12 passes per match), Tapsoba–Tah and Tapsoba–Xhaka (about 10 each). On the left, Hincapié–Grimaldo is a regular link (8 per match); on the right, Tah–Kossonou (8 per match).

**Left-sided first phase:** Grimaldo makes or receives 13% of own-half passes, Frimpong only 5%. Frimpong stays high near halfway as the outlet (59 exit receptions over the season), and the right side of the first phase goes through the right centre-back (Kossonou, 13%). The network chart shows the 11 players with the most minutes, so every position appears; an earlier version showed the 11 most-involved players, which left Frimpong out and confused the shape.

**Against Bayern (both matches):**
- **Tapsoba became the hub** (31% of passes, up from 20%) and Xhaka stayed at 27%. Bayern didn't take Xhaka out of the game, but the ball went through the centre-backs more.
- **The build-up started deeper:** the centre-backs' average pass position moved back by about 6 yards (x 39–40 to 33–34), and Hrádecký–Tapsoba became a main link (13 passes per match). Leverkusen were recycling the ball back to the goalkeeper.
- **Wirtz dropped in to help** (17% of build-up passes, up from 9%), with Wirtz–Xhaka a main link (11 per match). Leverkusen needed an extra player in the first phase to get out.
- *Check on video:* did Bayern's front line block passes into Xhaka and leave the centre-backs the ball? That would explain why Tapsoba saw so much of it.

**Xhaka's marking (1,959 receptions with 360 data):**
- **When he has 6m+ of space**, 16% of his next actions move the ball 10+ yards forward, and he almost never loses it (0.1%).
- **With an opponent within 6m, his forward play drops to under 4%**, whether tight (<3m) or at 3–6m. With an opponent within 3m, he loses the ball 5% of the time (versus 0.1% when free).
- **The effect is clear:** forward play is 12.7 points higher when he is free (90% interval 9.5 to 16.0).
- **No team kept him tight across a whole match.** His median space per match ranged from 3.8m to 7.3m, with only a weak link to Leverkusen's progression (r = 0.24). Bayern gave him 4.7–4.8m, about his usual 4.5m. Bayern's success did not come from man-marking Xhaka.

**What this means for the recommendation:**
1. **Don't let Xhaka receive free.** Keep someone within about 6m of him at all times; that alone cuts his forward passing by three-quarters. Getting tighter than 3m adds turnovers but is hard to sustain.
2. **That won't be enough on its own:** the ball will go through Tapsoba and Tah instead, and Wirtz will drop in to help. The press must be ready to jump onto the centre-backs and track Wirtz when he comes short (see the case study: Bayern's press *won the ball*).

**Limitations:** 360 frames only show players visible on camera, so the "nearest opponent" can be missed if they're off-screen (this would make Xhaka look freer than he was). Average positions mix pass and reception locations. The Bayern network is two matches.

## Phase 4 findings: the risk of pressing high (Figures 13, 14 and 18)
A **pressed build-up** = a Leverkusen possession from their own third with at least one opponent pressure in Leverkusen's defensive third (419 of 1,121 build-ups).

**Pressing slows them down (solid):**
- They got out of their own third 81% of the time when pressed high, against 94% when not.
- They reached the final third 45% of the time, against 60% (a 15-point drop; 90% interval 9 to 20 points).
- They lost the ball in their own third 4.8% of the time, against 2.6%.

**If they escape, the extra danger is not proven.** Once out of their third, pressed build-ups produced 2.39 xG per 100 against 1.80, but the 90% interval for the gap runs from -0.4 to +1.7. At level scores the gap reverses (1.47 vs 2.39). I first reported this as "33% more dangerous"; the uncertainty check showed that was not supported.

**Over the season, pressing cost more than it won, but game state explains most of it.**
- Overall: 3.3 xG won from the possession straight after a regain, 8.1 xG given up when Leverkusen broke the press (gap 4.9 xG; 90% interval 1.5 to 8.3).
- **Split by the score when the build-up started (Figure 18):**

| Score | Pressed build-ups | Won (xG per 100) | Gave up (xG per 100) | Final-third reach: pressed vs not |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Level | 173 | 1.4 | 1.2 | 38% vs 60% |
| Leverkusen leading | 210 | 0.3 | 2.8 | 50% vs 59% |
| Leverkusen trailing | 36 | 0.8 | 0.9 | 47% vs 70% |

- **At level scores, the press roughly broke even** (gap -0.3 xG; 90% interval -2.5 to +2.1) while cutting their final-third entries by 22 points. Removing spells after a red card changes nothing (all five reds were opponents').
- **The cost came when Leverkusen were already ahead** and opponents were chasing the game: 5.8 of the 8.1 xG given up.
- Per match, the press came out ahead in 9 of 34 matches and behind in 22. The clearest wins: Stuttgart at home (1-1, +0.80 xG), Darmstadt at home (0-2, +0.42) and Bayern at home (2-2, +0.16). Bochum at home (0-5) was the worst case, but Bochum were down to 10 men from the 14th minute.

### Overall recommendation, combining all of Phase 4
1. **While the score is level, press them high**, if your team can win the ball and keep it (Bayern and Frankfurt kept it from 12.5–15.7% of high pressures). It breaks even on chances and cuts their progression by a third.
2. **When chasing the game, don't throw bodies into a high press.** That is when Leverkusen punished it (2.8 xG given up per 100 pressed build-ups). Press in a compact block and keep cover behind.
3. **Always block passes into Xhaka and the inside channels:** a player stands between the ball and Xhaka, within 6 metres of him, and block the left half-space and centre, where their exits are worth most (Figures 6 and 12).
4. **Have a plan for the first pass after winning the ball:** Bochum won it as often as Bayern but gave it straight back.

**Limitations:** this is observational, and the level-score sample (173 pressed build-ups) leaves wide intervals. The reward counts the possession straight after any pressed build-up Leverkusen lost, including restarts. Single-match numbers are noisy; trust the season totals and the intervals.

## Phase 4 findings: how Leverkusen defend, and how to attack them (Figures 15–17)
Code: `src/defending.py`. Comparisons are against their opponents in the same 34 matches, not the whole league.

**Their press**
- They press high: 34% of their pressures come in the opponent's defensive third (their opponents: 19%). They allowed 18.3 passes per defensive action, against 21.2 for their opponents (lower = more aggressive).
- Per 90 minutes (players with 900+ minutes), Adli (25), Wirtz (24) and Hofmann (22) lead it, about half of it in the opponent's defensive third. Palacios (16) is the main midfield presser.
- **Counter-press:** after losing the ball they win it back within 5 seconds 18.5% of the time (opponents: 14.4%), rising to 22% when they lose it in the final third. The gap to their opponents in the final third (22% vs 19%) is within the noise (90% interval -0.3 to +5.9 points). This was measured from events, because StatsBomb possessions don't split quick regains, and my first possession-based attempt badly undercounted it.

**Where they concede** (293 non-penalty shots, 27.8 xG)
- **Set pieces are a major threat:** 8.7 xG (31%, second only to patient build-ups) and 10 of the 24 non-penalty goals they conceded, the most of any source, came within 10 seconds of a corner, free kick or throw-in.
- Patient build-ups made 11.6 xG. Winning the ball in midfield made 4.8. Fast breaks from deep made only 1.2: they rarely got caught on the counter from long range.
- **Attack the flanks, not the middle:** 76% of open-play attacks that ended in a shot went through a wide channel in Leverkusen's half, carrying 82% of the open-play xG. Attacks through the middle were worth 0.075 xG each, against 0.10–0.12 down the flanks (difference 0.034 xG per attack; 90% interval 0.004 to 0.063).
- **No weak side:** down Frimpong's side 5.2 xG (43 attacks), down Grimaldo's side 5.1 (49), both flanks 5.4 (51). The wing-back hypothesis (space behind Frimpong or Grimaldo specifically) is **not supported**; the flanks in general are.

**Beating their press** (1,290 opponent build-ups from their own third)
- Only 40% reached the final third (Leverkusen's own rate: 55%). When Leverkusen pressed the build-up high, that fell to 49% of exits reaching the final third, against 59% when not pressed.
- **Carry it out:** carries out of the defensive third added the most threat (23 xT per 1,000 build-ups) and reached the final third 63% of the time. Short passes added the least (16) and reached it only 47% of the time.
- Channel differences were small (14–23); their right half-space was slightly the best route, but treat that as a lean, not a rule.

**What this means for the game plan, with the ball**
1. **Carry, don't tiki-taka, out of their press:** use centre-backs who can step forward with the ball, or play longer past Wirtz and Hofmann. Short passes in front of their press lose most value.
2. **Go wide once through:** attack the flanks on either side and avoid forcing it through the middle.
3. **Make set pieces count:** they were the most common source of goals Leverkusen conceded (10 of 24 non-penalty goals).
4. **Secure the first pass after winning the ball in their half:** they win 22% of final-third losses back within 5 seconds.

**Limitations:** baseline = opponents in Leverkusen's own matches, who were often sitting deeper against them. "Wide channel" = any completed pass or carry ending wide in Leverkusen's half during the attack. The xT channel differences are small samples.

## Phase 4 findings: late goals (Figures 19–20)
Code: `src/late_goals.py`. Compares play from the 80th minute (stoppage time included) with minutes 1–79.

**The numbers:** 18 goals from the 80th minute, 3 conceded; 8–0 in second-half stoppage time. Those goals won 12 points: against Hoffenheim (A) +3; Augsburg (H), Leipzig (H) and Heidenheim (H) +2 each; Bayern (H), Dortmund (H) and Stuttgart (A) +1 each (opponent's venue, as elsewhere). They were behind at some point in 7 matches and lost none (2 wins, 5 draws).

**Why (tested, not assumed):**
- **Not luck:** 18 goals from 14.5 xG late, the same edge as the rest of the season (68 from 58.2).
- **Not more set pieces:** 10.4 corners and attacking free kicks per 90 late, 10.3 earlier.
- **Games opened up:** Leverkusen were ahead for about three-quarters of the late play. Opponents defended 9% further from their own goal but made 15% fewer pressures per 90. Leverkusen's open-play xG per 90 rose 26% and xG per open-play shot 12%; opponents' xG did not rise (-3%).
- **Shot cover didn't change:** about 0.8 defenders between shooter and goal in both phases (shot freeze frames), so the better chances came from shooting positions, not emptier boxes.
- **Substitutes:** scored 9 of 18 and were involved in 11, while playing 34% of late minutes. Above their share, but not conclusive (about 12% probability by chance).

**Two kinds of late goal:** 11 when already ahead (5 counter-attacks against teams pushing up); 7 when level or behind, 6 of them from set pieces or penalties (3 corners, 2 free kicks, 1 penalty). The second group won the points.

**For the game plan:** keep a rest defence when chasing late; when protecting a result, the danger is set pieces; plan our own late substitutions.

**Limitations:** 18 goals, and only 7 result-changing ones, is a small sample. Check the patterns on video.

## Quality checks (audit, 19 October 2026)
- Scores rebuilt from events (goals + own goals) match the official result in all 34 matches.
- Every key claim has a 90% interval from a cluster bootstrap by match (`src/uncertainty.py`, saved to `data/claim_intervals.csv`). Claims whose interval crosses zero are labelled as not proven.
- Units: StatsBomb pitch units are yards (120 x 80). Distances from 360 frames are converted to metres. Labels now say which.
- Pressing volume is per 90 minutes, from line-ups and substitutions.
- Pass-network percentages = share of completed passes a player made or received.
- Match labels everywhere are from the opponent's side: their venue, their goals first.
